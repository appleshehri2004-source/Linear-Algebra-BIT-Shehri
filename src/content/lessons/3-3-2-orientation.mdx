## Question

An area can't be negative. So what does it mean when a determinant is **−3**?

## Play

Drag the tip of <span class="ihat">**î**</span> (green) slowly up and over to the other side of <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span> (red). Watch the number in the patch: it shrinks to **0** when the two arrows lie on one line, then turns **negative**. The patch turns purple and striped, and says *orientation flipped*.

## Numbers

At the start, <span class="ihat">**î**</span> lands on <Formula tex="(2, 0)" /> and <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span> on <Formula tex="(1, 2)" />. The area is <Formula tex="4" />.

Press **Flat**: <span class="ihat">**î**</span> lands on <Formula tex="(0.5, 1)" />, exactly on the line through <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span>. The square is squashed into a line, so the area is <Formula tex="0" />.

Press **Flipped**: the columns are <Formula tex="(1, 1)" /> and <Formula tex="(2, -1)" />. The parallelogram has area 3, but it's been turned over:

<Formula display tex="\det \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = -3" />

## Explain

**The sign of the determinant says which way round the plane is.** Normally, <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span> is a quarter turn *anticlockwise* from <span class="ihat">**î**</span>. If after the transformation <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span> is *clockwise* from <span class="ihat">**î**</span>, the plane has been flipped over, like a page turned face down. Then the determinant is negative.

**The size is still the area.** <Formula tex="\det = -3" /> means "areas are multiplied by 3, and the plane is flipped". A mirror, for example, keeps areas the same but flips: its determinant is <Formula tex="-1" />.

**You can't flip without passing through 0.** As you drag <span class="ihat">**î**</span> across <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span>, the area changes smoothly. To get from positive to negative it has to pass through 0, the moment the two arrows lie on one line and the whole plane is squashed flat. That's why <Formula tex="\det A = 0" /> means the columns are *dependent* (Lesson 1.7.1).

## Go deeper

In 3D the same idea is the *right-hand rule*. Point your right hand's fingers along <span class="ihat">**î**</span> and curl them toward <span class="jhat">**ĵ**</span>: your thumb points along <span class="khat">**k̂**</span>. If after a transformation you need your *left* hand to do this, space has been flipped and the 3D determinant is negative.

Two flips make a "no flip". What's the sign of the determinant if you mirror the plane, then mirror it again across a different line?
