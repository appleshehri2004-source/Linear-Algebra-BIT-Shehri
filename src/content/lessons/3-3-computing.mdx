## Question

So far we found areas by looking at rectangles and slants. Is there a formula that works for **any** matrix? And what about volume in 3D?

## Numbers

For the matrix <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}" />:

- The box is <Formula tex="(a + b)(c + d) = 4 \times 3 = 12" />.
- The four triangles: <Formula tex="2 \times \tfrac{ac}{2} + 2 \times \tfrac{bd}{2} = 3 + 2 = 5" />.
- The two corner rectangles: <Formula tex="2 \times bc = 2" />.
- What's left: <Formula tex="12 - 5 - 2 = 5" />, and <Formula tex="ad - bc = 3 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1 = 5" />.

## Explain

**The 2×2 formula:**

<Formula display tex="\det \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = ad - bc" />

**Why it looks like that.** Expand the box: <Formula tex="(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd" />. Taking away <Formula tex="ac + bd + 2bc" /> leaves <Formula tex="ad - bc" />. The picture only works when all four numbers are positive, but the formula works always, including the sign.

**Reading it.** <Formula tex="ad" /> is the rectangle you'd get if there were no slant (<Formula tex="b = c = 0" />). The <Formula tex="bc" /> part measures how much the slants squash the parallelogram.

**In 3D: volume.** A 3×3 matrix turns the unit cube into a slanted box called a *parallelepiped*. Its determinant is the box's volume (negative if space is flipped). Press **Play**, then try the buttons.

To work out a 3×3 determinant by hand, go along the top row. Each number multiplies the 2×2 determinant left when you cross out its row and column, with signs <Formula tex="+, -, +" />:

<Formula display tex="\det \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = 2 \det \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} - 1 \det \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} + 0 = 2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 0 = 6" />

## Go deeper

When a 3×3 matrix has only zeros below the diagonal (a *triangular* matrix), its determinant is just the three diagonal numbers multiplied. In the 3D demo, the "Volume 6" matrix is like that: <Formula tex="2 \times 3 \times 1 = 6" />. Can you see why with the picture? (Hint: the shears don't change volume.)

The 3D determinant is also <Formula tex="\hat{\imath}' \cdot (\hat{\jmath}' \times \hat{k}')" />: the cross product gives the area of the floor made by the last two columns, as a vector standing up from that floor, and the dot product multiplies it by the height of the first column.
