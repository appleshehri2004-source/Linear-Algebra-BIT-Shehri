## Question

You walk along one arrow, then along a second arrow. Which single arrow takes you to the same place?

## Play

**Adding.** Drag the tips of <span class="vec">**v**</span> and <span class="vec2">**w**</span>.
<span class="vec2">**w**</span> starts where <span class="vec">**v**</span> ends. The thick arrow, <span class="sum">**v + w**</span>, goes straight from the origin to the end of the walk.

**Scaling.** Move the slider to change the number <Formula tex="k" />. Drag <span class="vec">**u**</span> too.

## Numbers

Adding works number by number: add the tops, then add the bottoms.

<Formula display tex="\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + 3 \\ 2 + (-1) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}" />

Scaling multiplies both numbers by <Formula tex="k" />:

<Formula display tex="2 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \qquad -1.8 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -3.6 \\ -1.8 \end{bmatrix}" />

## Explain

**Adding is walking.** To add <Formula tex="\vec{v} + \vec{w}" />, walk along <Formula tex="\vec{v}" />, then from there walk along <Formula tex="\vec{w}" />.
The sum is the arrow from where you started to where you stopped.
Each number in the sum is just the total movement in that direction, which is why we add top with top and bottom with bottom.

**Scaling is stretching.** Multiplying by a number <Formula tex="k" /> (a "scalar") stretches the arrow by that amount.
At <Formula tex="k = 2" /> it doubles. Between 0 and 1 it shrinks. A negative <Formula tex="k" /> flips the arrow to point the opposite way: at <Formula tex="k = -1.8" /> it flips and is 1.8 times as long.
At <Formula tex="k = 0" /> it shrinks to nothing.

## Predict

Does the order matter? Is <Formula tex="\vec{w} + \vec{v}" /> the same as <Formula tex="\vec{v} + \vec{w}" />?

<details>
  <summary>Reveal</summary>
  Yes, it's the same. Walking 3 east then 2 north lands you in the same place as 2 north then 3 east. With numbers: 1 + 3 = 3 + 1 and 2 + (−1) = (−1) + 2. In a picture, the two walks are the two sides of a parallelogram, meeting at the same corner.
</details>

## Practice

1. Work out <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}" />, then check it with the adding demo.
2. Which value of <Formula tex="k" /> turns <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}" /> into <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}" />?
3. Find a <Formula tex="\vec{w}" /> so that <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \vec{w}" /> lands back on the origin.

<details>
  <summary>Answers</summary>
  1. (−2, 4). 2. k = −1. 3. w = (−1, −2): the same arrow flipped, so the walk comes straight back.
</details>
