## Question

If you walk 3 steps east and 2 steps north, where do you end up?

## Play

Drag the yellow dot at the tip of the arrow. Try to land the arrow on each corner of the grid.

## Numbers

The column under the plane shows the arrow's two numbers. They change as you drag.
Tick "Snap to whole numbers" to make the tip jump from grid point to grid point.

## Explain

A vector has two faces, and they are the same thing.

**The arrow view.** A vector is an arrow that starts at the origin (the point where the axes cross).
What matters is how long it is and which way it points.

**The list-of-numbers view.** A vector is a column of numbers.
"3 steps east, 2 steps north" becomes

<Formula display tex="\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}" />

The top number is how far to go along <Formula tex="x" />, the bottom number how far along <Formula tex="y" />.

You can always swap one view for the other: the numbers tell you where the tip is, and the tip tells you the numbers.
Pictures help you see what is happening. Numbers are what a computer can calculate with.

**More numbers, more dimensions.** A column of 2 numbers is a vector in the plane, written <Formula tex="\mathbb{R}^2" />. A column of 3 numbers is an arrow in space, <Formula tex="\mathbb{R}^3" />. Nothing stops us at 3: a column of <Formula tex="n" /> numbers is a vector in <Formula tex="\mathbb{R}^n" />. We can't draw it any more, but every rule in this chapter works the same way, one number at a time.

## Predict

Which way does the vector <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}" /> point, and how long is it?

<details>
  <summary>Reveal</summary>
  Straight left (west), 2 steps long. The top number −2 means "go 2 steps the negative way along x", and the bottom 0 means "don't move up or down". Drag the arrow there to check.
</details>

## Practice

1. Drag the arrow to <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}" />. Which quarter of the plane is it in?
2. Where is the tip of <Formula tex="\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}" />? What does that arrow look like?

<details>
  <summary>Answers</summary>
  1. Top left: x is negative, y is positive. 2. At the origin. The arrow has no length at all: it is the zero vector.
</details>
