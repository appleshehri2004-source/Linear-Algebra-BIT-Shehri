## Question

How can one number tell you whether two arrows point the same way, opposite ways, or at right angles?

## Play

Drag <span class="vec">**v**</span> and <span class="vec2">**w**</span>. The thick arrow is the <span class="sum">**shadow**</span> of <span class="vec2">**w**</span> on <span class="vec">**v**</span>'s line: where <span class="vec2">**w**</span>'s tip lands if you drop it straight down onto the line (the dashed drop).

Watch the dot product <Formula tex="\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w}" /> under the plane. Make it positive, then zero, then negative.

## Numbers

**Length** comes from Pythagoras:

<Formula display tex="\left| \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} \right| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5" />

**Dot product:** multiply matching numbers, then add.

<Formula display tex="\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} = 1 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 4 = 11" />

**Angle:** the dot product also equals the two lengths times the cosine of the angle between the arrows.

<Formula display tex="\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w} = |\vec{v}|\,|\vec{w}| \cos\theta" />

**Shadow (projection)** of <Formula tex="\vec{w}" /> onto <Formula tex="\vec{v}" />'s line:

<Formula display tex="\text{proj}_{\vec{v}}\, \vec{w} = \frac{\vec{w} \cdot \vec{v}}{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{v}}\, \vec{v}" />

For example, the shadow of <Formula tex="(2, 2)" /> on the x-axis, the line through <Formula tex="(1, 0)" />:

<Formula display tex="\frac{(2,2) \cdot (1,0)}{(1,0) \cdot (1,0)} (1, 0) = \frac{2}{1} (1, 0) = (2, 0)" />

## Explain

**The sign tells you the direction.** Because <Formula tex="\cos\theta" /> is positive for angles under 90°, zero at exactly 90°, and negative above 90°:

- <span style="color: var(--positive)">**positive**</span>: the arrows point roughly the same way (angle under 90°),
- <span style="color: var(--zero)">**zero**</span>: they are at right angles (*perpendicular*, or *orthogonal*),
- <span style="color: var(--negative)">**negative**</span>: they point roughly opposite ways (angle over 90°).

**The shadow picture.** The dot product is the length of <Formula tex="\vec{w}" />'s shadow on <Formula tex="\vec{v}" />'s line, times the length of <Formula tex="\vec{v}" />. If the shadow points backwards along <Formula tex="\vec{v}" />, the dot product is negative. At a right angle there is no shadow at all, so the dot product is 0.

**Why this is useful.** Checking "is the dot product 0?" is the quickest way to test whether two directions are at right angles, and you'll use it all the time later.

## Predict

<Formula tex="\vec{v} = (2, 1)" /> and <Formula tex="\vec{w} = (-1, 2)" />. Without drawing, are they at right angles?

<details>
  <summary>Reveal</summary>
  Yes. <Formula tex="2 \cdot (-1) + 1 \cdot 2 = -2 + 2 = 0" />, so the dot product is 0 and the angle is 90°. Set the demo to these vectors to see w's shadow shrink to nothing.
</details>
