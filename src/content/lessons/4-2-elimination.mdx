## Question

Two equations you can solve by trying things. Three equations are harder, and a computer may need to solve a thousand. Is there a **recipe**, the same every time, that always works?

## Numbers

<Formula tex="x + 2y = 5" /> and <Formula tex="3x + 4y = 6" />:

<Formula display tex="\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 & 6 \end{array}\right]" />

1. **Clear below the first pivot.** The 1 in the top-left corner is the *pivot*. Take away 3 copies of row 1 from row 2 (<Formula tex="R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1" />), and the 3 under it becomes 0. Row 2 now says <Formula tex="-2y = -9" />: <Formula tex="x" /> is gone.
   <Formula display tex="\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 0 & -2 & -9 \end{array}\right]" />
2. **Make the pivot 1.** Multiply row 2 by <Formula tex="-\tfrac12" />: <Formula tex="y = \tfrac92" />.
   <Formula display tex="\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac92 \end{array}\right]" />
3. **Clear above it.** Take away 2 copies of row 2 from row 1 (<Formula tex="R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2" />): <Formula tex="x = -4" />.
   <Formula display tex="\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 0 & -4 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac92 \end{array}\right]" />

## Explain

Elimination uses just three moves:

- **Add a multiple of one row to another** (<Formula tex="R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1" />)
- **Multiply a row by a number that isn't 0** (<Formula tex="R_2 \to -\tfrac12 R_2" />)
- **Swap two rows**, if a pivot is 0

**Why the answer never changes.** If a point solves equation 1 and equation 2, it also solves "equation 2 minus 3 × equation 1". And every move can be undone (add the 3 copies back), so no new answers sneak in either. In the picture, a move swaps one line for a different line **through the same crossing point**.

**The recipe.** Going down, use each pivot to make zeros under it: now the last equation has just one unknown. Going back up, make each pivot 1 and clear the numbers above it. The left side becomes <Formula tex="I" />, and the right-hand column *is* the answer.

## Go deeper

**When the recipe gets stuck.** If a pivot is 0 and there's no row below to swap with, there's no unique solution. A row that becomes <Formula tex="0 = 0" /> means two equations were really the same (infinitely many solutions). A row like <Formula tex="0 = 3" /> means the equations disagree (no solution). Press **Random system** a few times: it only makes systems with one answer, but you can try <Formula tex="x + 2y = 3,\ 2x + 4y = 6" /> by hand.

**Each move is a matrix.** "<Formula tex="R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1" />" is the same as multiplying on the left by <Formula tex="E = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}" />. A whole elimination is a product of such matrices, and MIT Lecture 2 (linked below) builds on exactly this idea.

**This is what computers do.** Solving 1000 equations takes about <Formula tex="\tfrac13 \cdot 1000^3" /> multiplications, around 300 million: under a second.
