## Question

Multiplying two matrices means doing one transformation, then the other (Lesson 2.4). If <Formula tex="B" /> multiplies area by 2 and <Formula tex="A" /> multiplies area by 3, what does <Formula tex="AB" /> do to area?

## Numbers

With B = *Stretch y ×2* and A = *Stretch x ×3*:

- After step 1 (B), the area of the square is <Formula tex="\det B = 2" />.
- Step 2 (A) multiplies **every** area by <Formula tex="\det A = 3" />, including the area that's already 2. So the area becomes <Formula tex="3 \times 2 = 6" />.

Multiplying the matrices gives the same answer:

<Formula display tex="AB = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det(AB) = 6" />

Now try B = *Mirror*: <Formula tex="\det B = -1" />, and the final area is <Formula tex="3 \times (-1) = -3" />: flipped.

## Explain

<Formula display tex="\det(AB) = \det(A)\,\det(B)" />

**Why.** <Formula tex="B" /> multiplies every area by <Formula tex="\det B" />. Then <Formula tex="A" /> multiplies every area, whatever it is by now, by <Formula tex="\det A" />. Doing both multiplies by <Formula tex="\det A \times \det B" />. That's all there is to it. Proving it with the formula <Formula tex="ad - bc" /> takes a page of algebra; with pictures there is nothing to prove.

**Order doesn't matter here,** even though <Formula tex="AB \ne BA" /> in general: <Formula tex="\det A \det B = \det B \det A" />, because ordinary numbers can be multiplied in any order.

**Signs multiply too.** One flip and no flip: flipped (negative). Two flips: back the right way round (positive), just like <Formula tex="(-1)(-1) = 1" />.

## Go deeper

If <Formula tex="\det A = 0" />, then <Formula tex="\det(AB) = 0" /> for every <Formula tex="B" />: once the plane is squashed flat, nothing done before or after can give it area again. This is why a matrix with determinant 0 can't be undone. You'll meet *inverse* matrices in the next module.

If <Formula tex="A" /> can be undone by <Formula tex="A^{-1}" />, then <Formula tex="\det(A)\det(A^{-1}) = \det(\text{doing nothing}) = 1" />. So <Formula tex="\det(A^{-1}) = 1 / \det A" />.
