## Question

You have two arrows, <span class="vec">**v**</span> and <span class="vec2">**w**</span>. You may stretch each one as much as you like, then add them. Which points of the plane can you reach?

## Play

Move the sliders <Formula tex="a" /> and <Formula tex="b" />. The thick arrow is <span class="sum">**a·v + b·w**</span>, and its tip leaves a trail.
Press **Sweep** to try lots of <Formula tex="a" /> and <Formula tex="b" /> values quickly and fill in everything you can reach.

Then drag <span class="vec2">**w**</span> until it lies on the same line as <span class="vec">**v**</span> (try <Formula tex="\vec{v} = (2, 1)" /> and <Formula tex="\vec{w} = (-2, -1)" />) and sweep again.

<SpanTracer client:visible />

**One slider at a time.** Tick "Fix a" and move only <Formula tex="b" />, or press Sweep. The trail is a straight line, drawn dashed, through the tip of <Formula tex="a\vec{v}" /> and parallel to <span class="vec2">**w**</span>.

## Numbers

With <Formula tex="a = 2" />, <Formula tex="b = -1" />, <Formula tex="\vec{v} = (2, 1)" /> and <Formula tex="\vec{w} = (-1, 1)" />:

<Formula display tex="2 \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + (-1) \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}" />

## Explain

**Linear combination.** Scaling some vectors and adding them up, like <Formula tex="a\vec{v} + b\vec{w}" />, is called a *linear combination*. The numbers <Formula tex="a" /> and <Formula tex="b" /> are the weights.

**Span.** The *span* of <Formula tex="\vec{v}" /> and <Formula tex="\vec{w}" /> is the set of every point you can reach with some choice of <Formula tex="a" /> and <Formula tex="b" />. The sweep paints it in.

- Usually the span is **the whole plane**: two arrows pointing different ways can reach anywhere.
- If <Formula tex="\vec{v}" /> and <Formula tex="\vec{w}" /> lie on the same line, scaling and adding never leaves that line, so the span is **a line**.
- If both are the zero vector, the span is **a single point**, the origin.

**Why fixing a gives a line.** With <Formula tex="a" /> fixed, <Formula tex="a\vec{v}" /> is one fixed point. Adding <Formula tex="b\vec{w}" /> slides you from there along the direction of <Formula tex="\vec{w}" />, forwards or backwards. All those points form a straight line. Changing <Formula tex="a" /> moves that line sideways, and the lines together cover the plane.

## Predict

<Formula tex="\vec{v} = (1, 2)" /> and <Formula tex="\vec{w} = (2, 4)" />. Is the point <Formula tex="(3, 5)" /> in their span?

<details>
  <summary>Reveal</summary>
  No. <Formula tex="\vec{w}" /> is just <Formula tex="2\vec{v}" />, so every combination is some multiple of <Formula tex="(1, 2)" />: the bottom number is always twice the top. For <Formula tex="(3, 5)" />, 5 is not twice 3. Set the demo to these vectors and sweep: the trail misses that point.
</details>
